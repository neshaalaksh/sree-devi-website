h1: IVF and *ICSI* treatment
intro: In vitro fertilisation (IVF) combines eggs and sperm in the laboratory so that an embryo can be transferred to the uterus. In ICSI, a single sperm is injected into each egg as part of IVF.
sections:
- heading: 'IVF and ICSI: the *difference*'
  text: In IVF, eggs and sperm are placed together in the laboratory to fertilise. In ICSI (intracytoplasmic sperm injection), an embryologist injects one sperm into each egg. Your doctor will advise which is suitable, for example when there are concerns about sperm count or movement.
- heading: What the treatment *involves*
  text: '1. **Stimulation and monitoring.** Medicines help the ovaries produce eggs, with scans and blood tests to check the response.

    2. **Egg collection.** Eggs are collected from the ovaries, usually under anaesthesia.

    3. **Fertilisation.** Eggs and sperm are combined in the laboratory, by IVF or ICSI.

    4. **Embryo transfer.** An embryo is placed in the uterus.

    5. **Pregnancy test.** A test is done about two weeks after the transfer.


    Suitable extra embryos can be frozen for future use.'
- heading: Your consent and *rights*
  text: Under the Assisted Reproductive Technology (Regulation) Act, 2021, treatment needs your written informed consent. Your doctor will explain the procedure, its risks and what to expect before you decide. The Fertility Centre is registered as an ART clinic (Level II).
- heading: Cost and *estimates*
  text: Costs depend on the treatment plan and the medicines needed, so fees are not listed on this website. Ask for an estimate at your consultation.
